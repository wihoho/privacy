PlainLab privacy policy

Last updated November 23, 2026 (AI reading added)

This policy covers the PlainLab Android app (“PlainLab”), a blood test and lab result tracker. In short: your records stay on your phone. PlainLab has no account, no ads, no analytics and no tracking, and never sells or shares data. The only thing that ever leaves the phone is a report page you choose to read with AI, described below; it is processed and not stored. The app never asks for access to your location or contacts.

Information the app handles

  • Profiles you create (for example yourself and family members): a name.
  • Lab reports you add: exam date, lab or hospital name, notes, biomarker names, values, units, reference ranges, and the report photos or PDFs you attach.
  • Follow-up checkup reminders and your settings, such as the preferred unit system.

All of it stays on your phone, in the app's private storage. It is never sent to the developer or anyone else, except the report pages you choose to read with AI (next section).

Reading reports

When you scan a report, text recognition (OCR) first runs entirely on your phone with a recognition model built into the app; this works without internet.

Improve with AI (optional)

After a scan you can tap Improve with AI to read the values more accurately. Before anything is sent, a privacy screen shows each page: you choose which pages to send, and the top of each page (where your name, ID and barcode usually are) is blacked out by default; you can adjust or turn this off. Then only these are sent, over an encrypted connection, to our AI server: the selected page images with the black areas painted over, the text the phone read from the visible part of those pages (lines that look like a name, ID, date of birth, address or barcode are removed), and a random install number used only to count the daily limit (it is not linked to you, your device or any account). The server passes them to an AI model that reads the test names, values, units and ranges, sends the result back and keeps nothing: request and response contents are not logged or stored, and they are not used to train models. You review and can edit every value before saving. If you never tap Improve with AI, nothing is sent.

Photos and documents

You pick a report with Android's own photo picker or file picker, or take it with your camera app. The app only receives the single file you choose and keeps a copy in its private storage. It cannot see the rest of your photos or files.

Notifications

The app asks for permission to show notifications only when you set a follow-up reminder. Reminders are scheduled on your phone; nothing is sent anywhere.

Exports and backups

A doctor summary PDF, CSV spreadsheet or backup file is created only when you ask for one, and is sent only to the app you pick in the share sheet. Restoring a backup reads only the file you pick.

Ratings

After you have used the app a few times, Google Play may show its standard rating card. That card is provided by Google Play; the app itself sends nothing.

Android backup

If you have turned on backup in your phone's settings, Android may include the app's data in your own device backup. That backup is handled by Android and your Google account under their terms; the developer never receives it.

Storage and deletion

Deleting a report or profile in the app deletes it from the app's storage. Uninstalling the app removes all its data. Since we never receive your data, there is nothing for us to delete on our side.

Not medical advice

PlainLab is a record-keeping tool. Its high and low marks come from the reference ranges printed on your report or entered by you. It does not diagnose anything or give medical advice; talk to your doctor about your results.

What we don't do

  • We don't keep your personal or health information: report pages you send for AI reading are processed and discarded.
  • We don't sell or transfer user data to third parties.
  • We don't show ads or use advertising IDs.
  • We don't use analytics or crash-reporting services.
  • We don't use your location.

Children

The app is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect information from anyone.
